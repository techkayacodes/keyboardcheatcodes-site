This guide sorts keyboards by **what they're built for**, from the free one that came with your PC to the one court reporters use to type 225 words a minute. (Want to know about switches instead: optical, Hall-effect, Topre? See [every type of keyboard explained](/blog/every-type-of-keyboard-explained/).)

## 1. Membrane

The one that came free with your computer. Under every key, a soft rubber dome presses on a thin plastic sheet. It's cheap, quiet, and in basically every office on Earth. If typing feels like pressing a wet sponge, this is what you've got.

## 2. Laptop

Flat, thin and built in. Each key sits on a tiny plastic scissor that keeps it level, so the press is short and crisp. Perfect for working anywhere. Not so perfect when one crumb gets under the space bar.

## 3. Touchscreen

The keyboard you actually use most. No keys, just glass, so your phone guesses what you meant and fixes it for you. Fast for texting, rough for anything longer than a paragraph.

## 4. Mechanical

The one the internet is obsessed with. Every key has its own spring-loaded switch, so you get real feedback on every press. The keycaps and switches swap out, and a good one lasts for years. Your coworkers will notice.

## 5. Gaming

Mechanical, but built for speed. Gaming boards register a whole handful of keys pressed at once, and the newest ones use **magnets** so a key resets the instant you lift your finger. Plus a lot of RGB lighting, which won't make you play better but will make you feel like it does.

## 6. Ergonomic

Built to save your hands. The board is curved or split in two, so your wrists stay straight instead of bent outward all day. Expect a slow week while you adjust. If you type for a living, it's insurance for your wrists.

## 7. Macro pad

A tiny keyboard with just a few keys, each doing whatever you program: open an app, mute your mic, or run a ten-step shortcut in one press. Streamers and video editors swear by them. It's the closest thing to a real cheat code you can put on your desk.

## 8. Flexible (roll-up)

Soft silicone you can roll up, bend, wash and toss in a bag. Almost impossible to break, and almost impossible to type on fast. A travel gimmick, not a daily driver, but it survives coffee.

## 9. Projection

No keys at all. A tiny device beams a laser keyboard onto your desk and a sensor watches where your fingers tap. It looks like science fiction until you type on it and make a typo every other word.

## 10. Stenography

The fastest keyboard in the world. It has about 22 keys, and you press several at once to write a whole word or sound in one stroke. Court reporters have to hit **225 words a minute** just to get certified. Meanwhile, the rest of us are still hunting for the semicolon.

## Which one are you on?

Most people go from **membrane or laptop** to **mechanical**, and that's the upgrade you'll feel most. Whatever you type on, a handful of shortcuts will make you faster than any new keyboard: start with the [shortcut library](/shortcuts/).
